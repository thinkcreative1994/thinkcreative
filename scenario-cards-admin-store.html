SC-01Customer Service

Shelf price and cashier price mismatch

Role

Admin Store

Situation

A customer complains that the shelf price is lower than the cashier price. The cashier calls you because the line is getting longer.

Challenge

What should you do first?

  • AAsk the customer to pay the cashier price because the system is always correct.
  • BApologize, confirm the item and price difference, ask cashier to pause escalation politely, then check shelf tag/system evidence.
  • CAsk the customer to go back to the shelf and take a photo.
  • DTell the cashier to remove the item from the bill and handle it later.
Reflection: What exact words would I use in the first 30 seconds?
SC-02Store Operation

Opening checklist not completed

Role

Admin Store

Situation

It is 10 minutes before store opening. One section has not completed the opening checklist because the assigned staff arrived late.

Challenge

What is the best immediate action?

  • AOpen as usual and remind the staff later.
  • BDelay store opening until every checklist item is completed.
  • CIdentify the highest-risk unchecked items, assign temporary support, complete must-check items before opening, and record the issue for follow-up.
  • DAsk another department to sign the checklist on behalf of the staff.
Reflection: Which opening items are non-negotiable in my store?
SC-03Communication

Display change information missed

Role

Admin Store

Situation

HQ sent an updated display instruction yesterday evening. This morning, one store team is still following the old layout and says they never received the change.

Challenge

What should you do?

  • ABlame the team for not checking messages.
  • BForward the HQ message again without explanation.
  • CConfirm who received the message, summarize the exact change, clarify deadline/owner, and create a simple confirmation loop.
  • DIgnore because display is not urgent.
Reflection: How do I confirm that important updates were actually understood?
SC-04Admin Coordination

Approval deadline at risk

Role

Admin Store

Situation

A document that needs manager approval is due today. The manager is in meetings and has not replied. The requesting team is asking for status.

Challenge

What is the most professional response?

  • AWait until the manager replies because approval is not your responsibility.
  • BTell the requesting team the manager is too busy.
  • CSend a concise reminder to the manager with deadline, decision needed, risk if delayed, and proposed next step; update the requester with factual status.
  • DApprove it yourself to avoid delay.
Reflection: What information does my manager need to decide quickly?
SC-05New Store Opening

Task delay before store opening

Role

Admin Store

Situation

During new store opening preparation, training attendance confirmation is incomplete. Store opening is close, and manpower planning depends on this information.

Challenge

What should you do first?

  • AWait for all stores to reply naturally.
  • BCreate a short status list, identify missing names, contact owners directly, set a clear cut-off time, and report the risk early.
  • CSend a long email explaining why everyone should cooperate.
  • DRemove unconfirmed people from the list immediately.
Reflection: What launch task needs a daily status view?
SC-06Complaint Handling

Staff attitude complaint

Role

Admin Store

Situation

A customer says a staff member answered rudely. The staff member says the customer misunderstood and becomes defensive.

Challenge

What is the best first handling?

  • AAsk the staff to apologize immediately in front of everyone.
  • BListen to the customer, acknowledge the feeling, separate customer handling from staff coaching, then verify facts calmly.
  • CTell the customer that staff are busy and mistakes happen.
  • DAsk another staff member to decide who is right.
Reflection: How can I acknowledge emotion without admitting facts I have not checked?
SC-07Safety

Blocked aisle discovered

Role

Admin Store

Situation

You notice cartons temporarily placed in a customer aisle during a busy period. Staff say they will move them after finishing another task.

Challenge

What should you do?

  • ALeave them because the task is almost done.
  • BMove or assign immediate removal, then remind the team why aisle safety cannot wait.
  • CTake a photo and report later only.
  • DAsk customers to walk around the cartons.
Reflection: Which temporary habits in my store can become safety risks?
SC-08Process Compliance

Receiving quantity mismatch

Role

Admin Store

Situation

During goods receiving, the actual quantity is different from the delivery document. The team is busy and wants to adjust later.

Challenge

What should you advise?

  • AAccept the delivery first and fix the number after store closing.
  • BRecord the mismatch immediately, keep evidence, inform the responsible person, and follow receiving discrepancy process.
  • CAsk the delivery person to change the document manually.
  • DIgnore small differences if the item value is low.
Reflection: What evidence is required before I report a discrepancy?
SC-09Team Communication

Staff did not receive schedule change

Role

Admin Store

Situation

A staff member misses an updated shift because the schedule was changed in a chat group while they were off. The supervisor says the message was already sent.

Challenge

What is the best improvement?

  • ATell the staff to check messages more often.
  • BTell the supervisor not to change schedules anymore.
  • CClarify the official schedule-change channel, require acknowledgement for changes, and record the final schedule in one source of truth.
  • DLet each team create its own method.
Reflection: Where is the single source of truth for schedule changes?
SC-10Japanese Working Culture

Report lacks enough facts

Role

Admin Store

Situation

You report an operational issue to a Japanese manager: “The store is having many problems today.” The manager asks several follow-up questions and seems unsatisfied.

Challenge

What should the improved report include?

  • AMore emotion so the manager understands urgency.
  • BOnly the final conclusion, because managers are busy.
  • CFacts: what happened, when, where, impact, current action, risk, and what decision/support is needed.
  • DA full history of all related problems.
Reflection: How can I turn a vague issue into a decision-ready report?
SC-11Training Support

New staff does not know who to ask

Role

Admin Store

Situation

A new staff member says they received many documents but still does not know who to ask for daily operation questions.

Challenge

What is the best response?

  • ATell them to read the documents again.
  • BCreate or confirm a simple contact map: topic, first contact person, backup person, and escalation rule.
  • CAsk them to message everyone until someone replies.
  • DTell their manager that training failed.
Reflection: What are the top 5 question categories a new staff asks?
SC-12Improvement Mindset

Repeated small errors in reports

Role

Admin Store

Situation

The same minor errors keep appearing in weekly store reports: missing attachment, wrong date format, and unclear comment fields.

Challenge

What should you do?

  • ACorrect each report manually every week.
  • BSend a reminder saying everyone must be more careful.
  • CIdentify the repeated error pattern, update the template/checklist, show one correct example, and track whether errors reduce.
  • DEscalate all errors to senior management.
Reflection: What repeated correction can I turn into a checklist or template?

Answer Key & Debrief

SC-01 Shelf price and cashier price mismatch Answer: B

Apologize first, confirm facts, reduce pressure at cashier, check the shelf tag and POS/system information, then follow company rule for correction/reporting. The first behavior matters because the customer needs to feel heard before process correction.

Link: Customer service / price accuracy / escalation

SC-02 Opening checklist not completed Answer: C

Separate critical readiness from minor follow-up. Before opening, check safety, customer-facing readiness, cash/POS readiness if relevant, and visible operational risks. Record what was missed and follow up with owner after opening.

Link: Opening standard / risk control / ownership

SC-03 Display change information missed Answer: C

The issue is not only the message but the confirmation system. Clarify the change, owner, deadline, and proof of completion. Then improve the communication loop so future updates are acknowledged, not only sent.

Link: Information cascade / standardization

SC-04 Approval deadline at risk Answer: C

Escalation should help decision-making. Provide facts, deadline, impact, and proposed action. Do not hide delay, but do not create panic or overstep authority.

Link: Approval flow / reporting / accountability

SC-05 Task delay before store opening Answer: B

For launch work, visibility beats assumption. Build a status list, identify gaps, contact owners, set cut-off, and escalate early if the delay affects manpower or training readiness.

Link: Store opening / coordination / deadline control

SC-06 Staff attitude complaint Answer: B

Handle the customer emotion first without judging facts too early. Then collect information privately: what happened, what was said, who witnessed it, and whether service standard was followed. Coach behavior after facts are clear.

Link: Customer complaint / respectful communication

Answer Key & Debrief

SC-07 Blocked aisle discovered Answer: B

Safety risks should be removed immediately. After the area is safe, discuss why temporary placement still creates customer risk and what alternative holding area should be used.

Link: Safety / customer readiness

SC-08 Receiving quantity mismatch Answer: B

Quantity mismatch must be recorded at the point of discovery. Evidence and timely reporting protect the store from inventory, finance, and accountability issues.

Link: Receiving / inventory accuracy / compliance

SC-09 Staff did not receive schedule change Answer: C

For schedule changes, sending is not the same as confirming. There should be one official source, a clear acknowledgement rule, and a way to handle off-day communication fairly.

Link: Manpower / communication control

SC-10 Report lacks enough facts Answer: C

A good report helps the manager decide. Use concise facts and requested action: situation, impact, cause if known, countermeasure, deadline, and support needed.

Link: Hou-ren-sou / reporting / decision support

SC-11 New staff does not know who to ask Answer: B

Documents alone do not create support. New staff need a clear question route, especially in store operations where timing matters. A contact map reduces confusion and repeated mistakes.

Link: Onboarding / learner support / operation readiness

SC-12 Repeated small errors in reports Answer: C

Repeated small errors are usually system problems, not only attention problems. Improve the template, make the correct example visible, and track whether the fix works.

Link: Continuous improvement / standardization
